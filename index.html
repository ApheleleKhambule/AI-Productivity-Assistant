The problem

Repetitive admin quietly eats the working week.

Professionals repeatedly draft similar emails, rewrite scrappy meeting notes, decide what to do first, and dig through long documents for the one useful point. The work is necessary, but it is slow, inconsistent under pressure, and easy to get wrong when owners and deadlines get lost.

Who it helps

Operations and admin staff, team leads, business analysts and anyone whose job runs on email, meetings and deadlines.

The goal

Cut the time spent on routine writing and planning, without cutting the human judgement that keeps the output correct.

The solution

Five tools, one assistant.

The brief asks for at least three features. WorkMate delivers all five.

✉️

Smart Email Generator

Choose audience (client, manager, team), tone (formal, informal, persuasive) and purpose. Get a ready-to-send draft with a clear call to action.

2 · Safety scanDetects IDs, card numbers, phone numbers, passwords and warns first.
3 · Prompt builderAssembles a Role-Context-Task-Format-Constraints prompt.
4 · EngineDemo rules (no key) or a live LLM: Gemini, OpenAI or Claude.
5 · ReviewLabelled output, validation checklist, copy or download.

Prompt engineering

Every prompt follows R-C-T-F-C.

Role sets expertise. Context supplies only the facts the model may use. Task states one job. Format fixes the structure so output is predictable. Constraints block known failure modes such as invented owners, dates or statistics.

Each prompt was refined over three versions. For example, the meeting summarizer’s first version produced a loose paragraph and guessed missing owners. The final version adds exact headings, a “write Not stated” rule, a worked example and a low temperature.

Responsible AI

Useful, but never blindly trusted.

Transparency

Every output is labelled as AI-generated and shows whether the demo engine or a live model produced it. Live mode reveals the exact prompt used.

Human in the loop

Each result ends with a validation checklist (facts, owners, tone, confidentiality). The assistant drafts; the person decides.

Privacy by design

A scanner warns about IDs, card numbers, phone numbers and passwords before anything is sent. API keys stay in the browser tab only. There is no server and no stored data.

Accuracy guardrails

Prompts forbid invented facts and require “Not stated” or placeholders. Research output separates facts from inferences and adds a confidence note.

Fairness

Prompts require neutral, inclusive wording, and the interface reminds users that models can reflect bias in their training data.

Honest limits

The demo engine is rule-based, not an LLM, and says so. It cannot look things up or verify facts, and neither can a language model without sources: always check key claims.

Challenges & solutions

What went wrong, and what I did about it.

ChallengeSolution
AI summaries tend to guess missing owners and deadlines.Added a “Not stated” rule to the prompt, and the demo engine flags unassigned actions with a visible warning.
Generic plans over-book the day.The planner calculates available hours (with lunch and buffers), schedules by priority, and lists what could not fit.
Task wording such as “Team stand-up” has no priority signal.Unmarked tasks default to important so nothing is silently dropped. Only explicit low-value wording demotes a task.
API keys cannot be hidden in a static site.Bring-your-own-key, held only in session storage, plus a no-key demo mode. The README explains the server-side approach for production.
The app must work for anyone, even without an API key or internet.Built a transparent rule-based engine and covered it with automated tests.

What’s next
